**20260908-A — A vendor breach now confirmed to affect 9.5 million patients — the second-largest U.S. healthcare breach of 2026 — includes California providers, and it is the full scale behind last week's Northern Inyo report.** Aesto Health, an Alabama-based healthcare data-migration and archiving vendor, disclosed to federal regulators that its December 2025 breach affected 9,540,683 individuals across at least 29 healthcare-provider clients — the second-largest confirmed U.S. healthcare breach of 2026, behind only DentaQuest. The intrusion, on Aesto's Amazon Web Services infrastructure between December 2 and 18, 2025, was confirmed in scope on May 26, 2026, but individual patient letters did not go out until roughly August 21–25 — a gap of about eight months, well beyond the 60-day notification rule. Exposed data includes names, Social Security numbers, dates of birth, driver's license numbers, financial account numbers, and medical records. California providers are among those affected: Northern Inyo Healthcare District (in-AOR) filed with the California Attorney General on August 20, and SpineZone (now Livara Health) filed on August 25. No threat group has claimed the intrusion. *Why an SLTT organization should care: this is the vendor-accountability lesson at scale — a single third-party vendor sitting between dozens of hospitals and their patient data was breached, and California healthcare providers, including an in-AOR public hospital district, are among the affected. Public hospital districts and clinics should confirm whether they or their records vendors used Aesto, and the broader lesson holds: know which vendors hold your data, and require breach-notification and safeguard terms in contracts.*

## CTA TTPs

**20260908-E — Researchers demonstrated using Claude to port a working pre-authentication exploit from one industrial controller to another — a concrete look at AI-assisted attacks on operational technology.** Forescout's Vedere Labs disclosed that it used Anthropic's Claude (via Claude Code, given a terminal, the reverse-engineering tool Ghidra, and a live target) to port a pre-authentication remote-code-execution exploit from one WAGO programmable logic controller model to another, running attacker-supplied code on live hardware. The exploit targets a buffer-overflow flaw (CVE-2021-31886, CVSS 9.8) in an FTP service reachable before login, and the researchers said the AI solved a hard step — preserving the payload in memory — that had stalled manual attempts. The demonstration also showed the limits: it required sustained expert human steering, cost about $536 in AI usage over an 8.5-hour session, and one AI-generated payload permanently bricked the test device. WAGO has no fix available. *Why an SLTT organization should care: this is the AI-assisted-exploitation concern made concrete, on the exact class of device (PLCs) behind the water campaign — and WAGO controllers are used in water and building systems. The honest reading is balanced: AI lowered the effort to adapt an existing OT exploit across similar devices, but expert humans were still essential and the process was expensive and destructive. The takeaway is to treat "hard to exploit" control-system flaws as more significant than before, and to apply the same defenses: take control devices off the network edge, block unneeded services such as FTP, and segment.*
**Score:** [6.0] · cross-cutting adversary method (concrete instance of AI-assisted OT exploitation)
**Answers:** PIR-2 / PIR-3 (adversary method enabling attacks on SLTT sectors and technology)
*Sources: [The Hacker News, "Researchers Use Claude to Port Pre-Auth RCE Exploit From One PLC Model to Another," Sep 2, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/researchers-use-claude-to-port-pre-auth.html); [Cyber Security News, "Claude AI Builds Pre-Auth RCE Exploit for WAGO PLC," Sep 2, 2026](https://cybersecuritynews.com/claude-ai-builds-pre-auth-rce-exploit/).*

**20260908-G — SonicWall warns of two actively-exploited SMA 1000 VPN zero-days being chained for remote code execution — the third such chain on this product since December.** SonicWall released emergency hotfixes on September 1 for two zero-day vulnerabilities in its Secure Mobile Access (SMA) 1000 remote-access VPN appliances (models 6210, 7210, 8200v) after finding evidence of active exploitation. CVE-2026-83548 (CVSS 10.0) is a pre-authentication server-side request forgery flaw in the user-facing WorkPlace interface; CVE-2026-83549 (CVSS 7.8) is an OS-command-injection flaw in the admin console. Chained, they give an unauthenticated attacker remote code execution on the appliance. CISA added both to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a September 5 federal deadline; there is no workaround other than the hotfix. *Why an SLTT organization should care: SMA 1000 gateways sit at the network edge, reachable from the internet, and a pre-authentication remote-code-execution chain on a perimeter VPN can expose a whole network. Confirm whether any SonicWall SMA 1000 appliances are in use and apply the hotfix immediately; ask any IT provider to confirm. The repeated exploitation of this product line is itself a reason to prioritize it.*
