## NEWS

**20260903-A — CISA quantifies the scale of the water-sector attack campaign for the first time: more than 100 internet-exposed water systems targeted in July.** In guidance released August 26, CISA said it observed malicious activity targeting over 100 internet-exposed systems in the Water and Wastewater Systems sector during July, commonly via programmable logic controllers (the industrial computers running pumps, valves, and treatment) connected directly to a cellular modem. It is the first time the federal government has put a number on the campaign. The attacks hit mostly small, rural utilities across at least a dozen states; intelligence reporting suspects Iran-affiliated actors, though the government has not formally attributed it. There was no contamination of drinking water, but some intrusions modified controllers to disable shutdown processes and alarms — potentially creating unsafe conditions without alerting operators. This is a direct continuation of the five-agency Siemens S7 advisory. *Why an SLTT organization should care: water and wastewater is a core SLTT sector, and this campaign is hitting exactly the profile of many AOR utilities — small, rural, with control equipment reachable from the internet. CISA's priority guidance is concrete and low-cost: inventory internet-accessible systems, and route any remote access through a secure gateway, firewall, or VPN rather than connecting a controller directly to a cellular modem. Agencies that outsource water operations should confirm their operator has done this.*

**20260903-C — The White House banned foreign-made bulk-power grid equipment over cyber-backdoor concerns.** On August 26 the administration issued Executive Order 14420, declaring a national emergency and prohibiting the acquisition, import, or installation of foreign-produced bulk-power-system equipment — transformers, grid inverters, battery storage, generators, industrial control systems, protective relays, and associated software and remote-access — where the Department of Energy determines it poses a risk of sabotage or malicious remote operation. It applies to transactions after August 26 involving entities tied to roughly two dozen sanctioned countries, with focus on Chinese-made equipment; DOE has 120 days to publish rules. The order covers high-voltage bulk-power transmission and explicitly excludes local electricity distribution. *Why an SLTT organization should care: the direct scope is bulk-power transmission, so municipal utilities that run local distribution are largely not bound — but the principle is a procurement-governance lesson for any SLTT body running industrial-control equipment: foreign-made control hardware and its remote-access software can carry backdoors, so supply-chain scrutiny belongs in procurement. It is the policy response to the same critical-infrastructure threat behind the water campaign.*

## CTA TTPs

**20260903-D — Researchers demonstrated using Claude to port a working pre-authentication exploit from one industrial controller to another — a concrete look at AI-assisted attacks on operational technology.** Forescout's Vedere Labs disclosed on September 2 that it used Anthropic's Claude (via Claude Code, given a terminal, the reverse-engineering tool Ghidra, and a live target) to port a pre-authentication remote-code-execution exploit from one WAGO programmable logic controller model to another, running attacker-supplied code on live hardware. The exploit targets a buffer-overflow flaw (CVE-2021-31886, CVSS 9.8) in an FTP service reachable before login, and the researchers said the AI solved a hard step — preserving the malicious payload in memory — that had stalled manual attempts. The demonstration also showed the limits: it required sustained expert human steering, cost about $536 in AI usage over an 8.5-hour session, and one AI-generated payload permanently bricked the test device. WAGO has no fix available. *Why an SLTT organization should care: this is the AI-assisted-exploitation concern made concrete, on the exact class of device (PLCs) behind the water-sector campaign — and WAGO controllers are used in water and building systems. The honest reading is balanced: AI lowered the effort to adapt an existing operational-technology exploit across similar devices, but expert humans were still essential and the process was expensive and destructive. The takeaway is to treat "hard to exploit" control-system flaws as more significant than before, and to apply the same defenses — take control devices off the network edge, block unneeded services such as FTP, and segment control networks.*
**Score:** [6.0] · cross-cutting adversary method (concrete instance of AI-assisted OT exploitation; ties to items A and E)
**Answers:** PIR-2 / PIR-3 (adversary method enabling attacks on SLTT sectors and technology)
*Sources: [The Hacker News, "Researchers Use Claude to Port Pre-Auth RCE Exploit From One PLC Model to Another," Sep 2, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/researchers-use-claude-to-port-pre-auth.html); [Cyber Security News, "Claude AI Builds Pre-Auth RCE Exploit for WAGO PLC to Execute ARM Shellcode Without Credentials," Sep 2, 2026](https://cybersecuritynews.com/claude-ai-builds-pre-auth-rce-exploit/).*

**20260903-E — Researchers find attackers bypass AI-assistant safety guardrails with almost no technique — often just by claiming they own the target.** Cisco Talos examined prompt logs and artifacts pulled from threat-actor endpoints running AI coding assistants (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini) and found that model safeguards offer little resistance to anyone willing to reframe a request. Claiming to own the servers being targeted, or asserting the work is a capture-the-flag or bug-bounty exercise, was frequently enough to free the assistant to hunt for and exploit vulnerabilities — with no proof ever required. What stood out was the absence of any sophisticated method. Separate academic research the same month showed commercial AI guardrail products can themselves be evaded at high rates. *Why an SLTT organization should care: the significance is the falling skill floor — AI assistants are being coaxed into doing reconnaissance and exploitation for attackers who lack deep expertise, which widens the pool of people who can target a small agency. This is context for the rising volume of attacks (like the water campaign) rather than a specific flaw to patch; the takeaway is that "we're too small to interest a skilled attacker" is a weaker assumption than it used to be.*
**Score:** [10.14] · broad adversary-capability trend
**Answers:** PIR-4 (broad/national development with material relevance to SLTT defenders)
*Sources: [The Register, "Bypassing AI guardrails is so easy a script kiddie can do it," Aug 2026](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/08/04/bypassing-ai-guardrails-is-so-easy-a-script-kiddie-can-do-it/); [Security Boulevard, "AI Guardrails Aren't Enough," Aug 11, 2026](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/08/ai-guardrails-arent-enough-when-the-model-behaves-perfectly-and-the-breach-happens-anyway/).*

## LATEST ATTACKS OR RISKS

**20260903-F — CISA adds six actively-exploited flaws to its catalog, led by a Citrix NetScaler gateway bug under live attack.** On August 26 CISA added six vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, citing active exploitation, and set federal remediation deadlines. The headline flaw is CVE-2026-8452 in Citrix NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway (a memory flaw that can crash the appliance configured as an SSL VPN or gateway); researchers observed attackers dropping web shells and running discovery commands, with 36 exploitation attempts from a dozen IPs in twelve days. The set also includes a Microsoft SQL Server remote-code-execution flaw and older Linux and Red Hat bugs, several tied to a Chinese group targeting web servers. *Why an SLTT organization should care: Citrix NetScaler gateways are internet-facing remote-access appliances, and a perimeter-device flaw under active attack can expose a whole network. Confirm whether any NetScaler/Citrix gateways or SQL Server instances are in use, apply the updates, and ask any IT provider to confirm patching against the KEV list.*
**Score:** [4.5] · Tier 3 exploited flaw in SLTT-common technology · ×1.5 exploited/active · force-surfaced (M2)
**Answers:** PIR-3 (actively-exploited vulnerability in SLTT-common technology)
*Sources: [The Hacker News, "CISA Adds Six Exploited Flaws to KEV, Including NetScaler, Linux, and SQL Server Bugs," Aug 27, 2026](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/cisa-adds-six-exploited-flaws-to-kev.html); [CISA, "CISA Adds Six Known Exploited Vulnerabilities to Catalog," Aug 26, 2026](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/08/26/cisa-adds-six-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog).*

**20260903-G — CISA warns of continued active exploitation of on-premises SharePoint, now spanning six vulnerabilities.** CISA updated its SharePoint hardening alert to reflect ongoing active exploitation of on-premises SharePoint Server, now covering six vulnerabilities (most recently CVE-2026-55040, added to KEV on August 18). The flaws affect all supported on-premises versions and enable remote code execution plus post-exploitation activity — stealing the server's IIS machine keys and using deserialization for persistence. *Why an SLTT organization should care: on-premises SharePoint is widely used across local government and school districts, and it is a repeatedly-targeted platform. Because attackers steal machine keys for persistence, patching alone may not fully evict them — CISA's hardening guidance (including rotating machine keys) matters. Confirm SharePoint is patched, keep it off the public internet where possible, and follow the hardening steps.*
**Score:** [4.5] · Tier 3 exploited flaw in SLTT-common technology · ×1.5 exploited/active · force-surfaced (M2)
**Answers:** PIR-3 (actively-exploited vulnerability in SLTT-common technology)
*Source: [CISA, "CISA Urges SharePoint Hardening After New Exploitations," updated Aug 2026](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/07/14/cisa-urges-sharepoint-hardening-after-new-exploitations).*

**20260903-H — SonicWall discloses a critical NetExtender VPN-client flaw that lets an attacker write files as root.** SonicWall advisory SNWLID-2026-0013 (August 25) disclosed two vulnerabilities in the NetExtender Linux client. The more severe, CVE-2026-66152 (CVSS 8.8), is a path-traversal flaw in how the client handles an OPSWAT tarball: an attacker can place files outside the intended directory, and because the operation runs as root, this allows arbitrary file writes as root — a serious privilege-escalation path. A second flaw (CVE-2026-66153, CVSS 7.0) is a symlink-following issue in the auto-upgrade process. No workaround is available; the fix is NetExtender 10.3.6 or later. *Why an SLTT organization should care: SonicWall remote-access products are common in small agencies, and a root-level file write on the VPN client is a foothold-to-full-control path. Confirm NetExtender Linux clients are updated to 10.3.6 or later, and ask any IT provider to confirm.*
**Score:** [4.5] · Tier 3 flaw in SLTT-common technology
**Answers:** PIR-3 (vulnerability in technology commonly deployed in SLTT environments)
*Source: [Cyber Security News, "SonicWall NetExtender Vulnerabilities Allow an Attacker to Write Arbitrary File as Root," Aug 25, 2026](https://cybersecuritynews.com/sonicwall-netextender-vulnerabilities/).*

**20260903-I — Palo Alto patches a batch of GlobalProtect VPN-client flaws, the most serious granting SYSTEM-level access.** Palo Alto Networks disclosed multiple vulnerabilities in its GlobalProtect VPN client (CVE-2026-0294 through CVE-2026-0299). The most significant, CVE-2026-0299 (CVSS 8.5), lets a local user gain SYSTEM privileges on Windows or root on macOS and Linux; others include a certificate-validation flaw enabling man-in-the-middle interception and security-inspection bypasses. Fixes are rolling out across supported versions through late August; Palo Alto reports no known active exploitation. *Why an SLTT organization should care: GlobalProtect is a widely used remote-access VPN, and a local-to-SYSTEM escalation on the client is a meaningful post-foothold risk. Confirm GlobalProtect clients are on the fixed versions, and ask any IT provider to confirm.*
**Score:** [4.5] · Tier 3 flaw in SLTT-common technology
**Answers:** PIR-3 (vulnerability in technology commonly deployed in SLTT environments)
*Source: [SecNews, "Palo Alto: New GlobalProtect and Prisma Access Agent vulnerabilities," Aug 2026](https://www.secnews.gr/en/726319/palo-alto-eypatheies-globalprotect/).*
