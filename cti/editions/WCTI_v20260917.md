## NEWS

**20260916-A — A cyberattack on Springfield, Massachusetts Public Schools closed the district for a week and has now been confirmed by the FBI as a data breach affecting staff and students.** Springfield Public Schools — Massachusetts's second-largest district (23,000+ students, 2,000+ staff) — was hit by a cyberattack around Labor Day that blocked access to email, phones, computers, and third-party platforms holding student medical records, transportation, and food-service data, forcing a four-day closure (schools reopened September 14). On September 15–16 the FBI informed the district that data was breached, including staff and student information; officials said staff Social Security numbers may be involved (not confirmed), while students' SSNs are typically not kept in district files. The superintendent classified it a "Level 4" incident (the highest). FBI, Massachusetts State Police, and local police are investigating; free credit monitoring is being arranged for staff. *Why an SLTT organization should care: K-12 school districts are a core SLTT sector, and this is the profile California districts share — heavy reliance on third-party platforms holding sensitive student and staff data, and operations that stop when systems go down. A particularly instructive detail: nurses lost access to student medical records (medications, allergies), a patient-safety impact beyond data loss. The takeaways are foundational: offline/paper continuity plans (Springfield taught on paper for a week), tested backups, multifactor authentication, and knowing which third-party platforms hold your students' data.*
**Score:** [4.0] · Tier 2 SLTT-sector targeting (K-12 education) · force-surfaced (M3)
**Answers:** PIR-2 (threat activity targeting an SLTT sector — education)

## LATEST ATTACKS OR RISKS

**20260916-G — Russia's Sandworm and a Qilin ransomware affiliate are actively exploiting critical Cisco firewall-management flaws.** Cisco Talos disclosed (September 9) active, widespread exploitation of two vulnerabilities in on-premises Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC): CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0), an authentication-bypass that lets an unauthenticated remote attacker gain root, and CVE-2026-20316 (CVSS 5.3), static credentials usable for low-privilege access. Talos found three intrusion clusters — one unattributed (web shells + credential theft), one linked to Sandworm (Russian GRU, deploying the Cyclops Blink implant), and one linked to a Qilin ransomware affiliate. CISA added CVE-2026-20079 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. FMC is the management plane for Cisco firewalls, so a compromise gives attackers the network's topology, the ability to pivot to managed devices, and the ability to disable detection. Only on-premises FMC is affected (cloud FMC, ASA, and FTD are not). *Why an SLTT organization should care: this is a firewall-management platform under active attack by both a nation-state and a ransomware crew, and compromising it means losing the perimeter itself. Larger SLTT and county IT shops running on-prem Cisco FMC should apply the hotfixes immediately (do not wait for a broader release), keep management interfaces off the public internet, and check for the indicators Talos published.*
**Score:** [7.61] · Tier 3 exploited flaw in SLTT-common technology · ×1.5 exploited/active · force-surfaced (M2)
