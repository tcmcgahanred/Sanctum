**20260820-A — A multi-state wave of local-government cyberattacks is disrupting municipal services; a California city's public-safety systems are among those hit.** Suisun City (Solano County, ~30,000 residents) declared a state of emergency on August 8 after malicious software compromised its IT systems early on August 7. According to the city's official statement, the attack hit "critical public safety operations, including 911 routing, police and fire dispatch, records and City services," and the city shut down its entire network to contain the incident and preserve evidence. 911 service was not lost: dispatchers failed over to Solano County's backup dispatch center and continued answering calls, and officials said there was no imminent threat to the public. Officials have not identified the malware family, called the incident ransomware, disclosed how it entered, or confirmed whether data was taken; however, the City Council met in closed session on August 12 to discuss "demands" from those behind the attack, indicating an extortion component consistent with ransomware. The incident is reported alongside a broader wave of local-government and water-utility cyberattacks across several states. *Why an SLTT organization should care: this is the core audience — a small California city whose public-safety IT was disrupted enough to force a dispatch failover. Suisun is in Solano County (near-AOR), but the profile reads directly onto AOR municipalities. Actionable posture: a tested failover/continuity plan for public-safety dispatch, offline backups, and multifactor authentication on administrative access.*
**Score:** [10.4] · T1 AOR-direct (California as subject of an incident) · ×1.3 ransomware vs public-sector/CI

**20260820-F — Unpatched GeoServer zero-day (unauthenticated SQL injection to remote code execution) under active exploitation.** Researchers disclosed an unauthenticated SQL injection flaw in GeoServer's jsonArrayContains filter function, reachable through the publicly accessible mapping services (OGC WMS/WFS) the software exposes; under certain database configurations it can lead to remote code execution on the database host. Disclosed August 12 and, per watchTowr, already seeing active exploitation attempts; as of reporting it remained unpatched with no CVE assigned. "Unauthenticated SQL injection" means an attacker who has not logged in can inject database commands through the exposed service. *Why an SLTT organization should care: GeoServer is widely used by government and utilities to publish maps and geospatial data, and an internet-exposed instance is directly reachable with no credentials. Confirm whether any GIS/mapping services run GeoServer, restrict public access to the affected services, and watch for the vendor patch.*

## CTA TTPs

**20260820-K — Akira ransomware intrusion entered through a SonicWall VPN with no multifactor authentication; the encryption failed but data was still stolen.** Researchers at Huntress detailed an early-August Akira ransomware intrusion that began with a password-guessing ("credential spraying") attack against a SonicWall SSL VPN that had no multifactor authentication. Within hours the attacker reached the domain controller over Remote Desktop, stole files to cloud storage for extortion, and installed remote-access software for persistence. To blind the defenses, the attacker rebooted the machine into Windows "Safe Mode," a diagnostic startup mode in which third-party security tools and Microsoft Defender's real-time protection do not run. The ransomware payload then failed to encrypt files (it ran out of memory in Safe Mode) — but the data theft had already succeeded, so the extortion threat remained. *Why an SLTT organization should care: the entire intrusion hinged on a remote-access VPN with no multifactor authentication — the single control that would most likely have stopped it. The encryption failure was luck, not a defense to rely on. Foundational asks: multifactor authentication on every VPN and remote-access account, endpoint detection on every host, and alerting on bursts of failed VPN logins.*
**Score:** [10.14] · T2 SLTT-sector targeting · ×1.5 low-maturity tech · ×1.3 supply-chain · ×1.3 ransomware vs public-sector/CI
*Sources: [Infosecurity Magazine, "Akira Affiliate Crashes Ransomware After Attempting EDR Evasion," Aug 13, 2026](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/akira-affiliate-crashes-ransomware/); [BleepingComputer, "Akira hackers disable EDR with Safe Mode, steal data but fail to encrypt," Aug 13, 2026](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/akira-hackers-disable-edr-with-safe-mode-steal-data-but-fail-to-encrypt/).*

**20260820-L — New Mirai-based Linux botnet "Evooo1Bot" hijacks internet-exposed edge devices.** A new Mirai-derived Linux botnet, "Evooo1Bot," exploits known vulnerabilities to turn internet-exposed edge devices (routers and similar) into proxies, with a reported 16 DDoS methods and SOCKS5 proxy capability. *Why an SLTT organization should care: small agencies often run consumer or low-cost routers that are exactly this botnet's targets, and a hijacked device becomes attack infrastructure. **20260820-M — Criminals hijacking public Wi-Fi DNS settings to steal credentials.** Attackers are compromising public Wi-Fi equipment at hotels, conference centers, and similar venues and altering the network's DNS settings (which translate website names to addresses) to silently redirect users to fake login pages that harvest passwords. *Why an SLTT organization should care: staff traveling for conferences or working remotely are the target, and the redirection is invisible to the user. The practical defense is user awareness and a corporate VPN for any work done on public networks, so traffic is protected even on a hostile Wi-Fi.*
**Score:** [1.0] · T4 broad/national — scored low; rescued by analyst as a plain-language awareness item for traveling staff.
