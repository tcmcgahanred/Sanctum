## NEWS

**ADHOC-A — A cyberattack on medical-device maker Boston Scientific has disrupted its ability to manufacture and ship products, a supply-chain risk for the healthcare providers that depend on it.** Boston Scientific, a Massachusetts-based medical-device manufacturer, disclosed in an August 26 securities filing that it detected a cybersecurity incident on August 25 that caused a global network outage. The company said the incident has disrupted access to certain information systems and business applications, including its ability to manufacture products and to process and ship customer orders; staff at its Cork, Ireland facilities were sent home. In updates through August 30, the company said it found no unauthorized activity since August 25, that the impact appears limited to certain on-premises systems, and that cloud systems were not affected. It stated there is no impact to the function of existing implantable cardiac rhythm devices or to remote monitoring of previously-activated devices, but that new remote-monitoring activations for some cardiac monitors are affected. The attacker, motive, and method have not been disclosed, and the company has not characterized the incident as ransomware. *Why an SLTT organization should care: this is a supply-chain and continuity concern, not an attack on an SLTT entity. California public hospital districts and other healthcare providers in the AOR depend on Boston Scientific devices (cardiac, neurology, oncology), and a disruption to the company's ability to manufacture and ship can delay procedures — reporting notes a device that misses its ship date can mean a cancelled surgery. The actionable posture is vendor and continuity governance: identify whether your facility or its providers rely on Boston Scientific products, ask about order and delivery status, and confirm clinical continuity plans for affected device lines. This is the same vendor-dependency lesson as recent third-party healthcare breaches — the risk to your organization can arrive through a supplier, not your own network.*
